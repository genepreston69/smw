# Capitalized Jobs

`/capitalized-jobs`, `/capitalized-jobs/already-capitalized`. (`/capitalized-labor…` and `/capitalized-materials` redirect here — `next.config.ts`.)

Code: `src/lib/capitalizedJobs.ts`, `src/lib/capitalizedJobsServer.ts`, `src/lib/capitalizedLabor.ts` (job rule and period), `src/lib/alreadyCapitalized.ts`.

## Capitalized Jobs

- `/capitalized-jobs` lists the **direct cost posted to non-billable (EQP) and intercompany jobs**, one row per job with a column per Direct Cost account — Labor (710 Labor Cost), Contract service (720 Contract Service), Fuel & oil (839 Fuel & Oil), Material (705 Material Cost), Freight (837 Freight) — then Total cost, Already capitalized, Awaiting review (total − capitalized) and Benefit allocation. It replaced the separate Capitalized Labor and Capitalized Materials pages.
- Which jobs qualify is `capLaborBucket` in `src/lib/capitalizedLabor.ts` (EQP → Non-Billable, sister-company customer → Intercompany; transportation and Precision Paint's work for Superior Marine never qualify). It takes the sister-company setting as a required argument.
- **Which lines count, and in which column, lives only in the SQL view `cap_job_lines` (migration `0044`)**, which the page, its line drill-down, and both exports read:
  - Bills, purchases, vendor credits and journal entries count; time entries never do. A line on a balance-sheet account never counts (withholdings and other payroll liabilities, a purchase straight into inventory or a fixed asset).
  - The column (`kind`) comes from the line's account in the chart: `cap_job_accounts` gives every account its own or its nearest parent's column, and `cap_job_account_kind(number, name)` is the one test — the account number decides (QuickBooks' number, or the number the name starts with), and a chart numbered differently is matched by name. **Adding or changing a Direct Cost account is a new migration that replaces this function**, plus the labels in `src/lib/capitalizedJobs.ts`.
  - `cap_job_accounts` is intentionally **not** `security_invoker`: the chart is admin-only but this page is open to every signed-in user, and with the caller's rights a non-admin would silently lose every line placed by its account. It exposes only account id → column. `tests/db/capitalizedJobs.test.ts` checks a non-admin sees the same lines.
  - A line whose account the chart no longer has is judged by the account name it carries (`cap_job_category_kind`).
  - A journal line on a wage or employer-payroll-tax expense account booked outside 710 is still Labor (`cap_labor_account`, migration `0032`) — the rule Capitalized Labor always used, so payroll never drops out. An account under one of the five decides first.
  - An item line that can't be traced to an account (frozen pre-2025 history) counts as Material.
  - **Expenses on any other account are not counted.** (Capitalized Materials counted every expense; this page is the five Direct Cost accounts only.)
- A journal line is *capitalized* when its entry debits a Fixed Asset / Other Asset account (`job_costs.je_debits_asset`) — the asset varies by equipment and job, so detection is by account type, never name; everything else is *cost posted*, net of refunds, vendor credits and reversals. Already capitalized is one column across all five accounts.
- `syncJobCosts` stores the account facts per line (`qb_account_id`, `account_classification`, `je_debits_asset`; `isCapitalAssetAccount` and `expenseLineAccountId` in `src/lib/quickbooks.ts`); migrations `0030` and `0036` backfilled frozen pre-2025 rows.
- It is viewed for **one period at a time — a calendar year, a single month, or a from/to month range** (default: the current year to date; history back to Jan 2023; UTC dates, never past today). The URL is the state (`?year=2025`, `?month=2026-03`, `?from=2025-07&to=2026-06`; a range beats a month beats a year, anything invalid falls back to the default) plus `?tab=nonbillable|intercompany`, and `capLaborPeriod` in `src/lib/capitalizedLabor.ts` is the one parser.
- **One loader**: the page, the CSV (`/api/export/capitalized-jobs`) and the workbook (`/api/export/capitalized-jobs-workbook`: Jobs, By Month, Cost Lines) all read through `loadCapJobs` in `src/lib/capitalizedJobsServer.ts` — which buckets the jobs, then reads lines only for the candidate jobs, 100 job ids per request on `job_costs_job_date_idx` — and roll up with the helpers in `src/lib/capitalizedJobs.ts` (`capJobRows`, `capJobTotal`, `capJobsByMonth`), so screen and file match. The job drill-down (`getCapJobLines`) re-parses the page's period params.
- `PeriodPicker` (`src/app/(app)/capitalized-jobs/`) is the shared year-pills / month / range control (plain links and GET forms).
- For multi-month periods the page shows a **By month** card and the workbook a *By Month* sheet (`capJobsByMonth`; follows the active tab on screen, all candidates in the file); each month row links to that month alone.
- The benefit allocation column is read straight from `job_benefit_allocation_cache` for the period's months (month-grain, so a partial month counts in full). A failed read is a banner on the page and a failed download, never blank cells.
- If migration `0044` is not applied yet the page shows a banner naming it (`capJobsErrorMessage`).
- Pre-2025 frozen rows predate vendor-credit import and refund signing, so 2023–2024 non-labor figures are an upper bound (the page's methodology says so).
- `cap_labor_lines` (`0030`/`0032`), `cap_material_lines` and `cap_material_account` (`0036`) are no longer read by the app. They were left in place so the old pages kept working during the rollout; a later migration can drop them.

## Already Capitalized

- `/capitalized-jobs/already-capitalized` (sidebar Capitalized Jobs → Already Capitalized, admin-only because it reads the ledger) is the schedule of **labor** already moved to asset accounts. It is labor-only: an entry that also capitalizes materials shows them in its expanded lines but only labor is totaled.
- The labor-account test is one SQL function, `cap_labor_account(name, classification)` (migration `0032`): wages and the employer's share of payroll taxes on the expense side; withholdings and every other payroll liability never count.
- `capitalized_labor_entries(p_from, p_to)` (migration `0032`; `0033` scopes it to the org so `gl_lines_date_idx` range-scans just the window instead of the whole ledger) returns, as one JSON row, every general-ledger line of the journal entries that debit a Fixed Asset / Other Asset account and, net, credit labor — so it also catches capitalization credits with no job tag — and `src/lib/alreadyCapitalized.ts` shapes them into entries and a by-asset rollup (labor split pro-rata when an entry debits several assets) for both the page and its export (`/api/export/already-capitalized`, shared loader `loadCapSchedule`).
- It takes the same period selection as Capitalized Jobs (`capLaborPeriod`); a long range reads more of the ledger, and a timeout renders as a banner via `capScheduleErrorMessage`. Multi-month periods get a By month card and sheet (`capScheduleByMonth`).
